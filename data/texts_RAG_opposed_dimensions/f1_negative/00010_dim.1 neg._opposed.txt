**CANDIDATE RESPONSE**:

Thank you for the question. The Constitution is a living document, one that has served us well by providing a framework flexible enough to adapt to changing societal norms while grounded in timeless principles. Amendments to the Constitution should be considered only when they address issues of profound and enduring significance to our democracy. Proposals like those for balanced budgets, school busing, school prayer, abortion, and gun control, while important, often reflect specific political debates rather than the broad principles that warrant constitutional permanence.

When we talk about issues like abortion or school prayer, we must prioritize individual autonomy and personal responsibility. Decisions about reproductive health or the role of prayer in education are deeply personal and reflect the diversity of values in our nation. These are not matters that should be dictated by federal mandates or enshrined in the Constitution. Instead, they should remain within the realm of individual choice and local governance, guided by respect for personal freedoms and cultural pluralism.

Regarding gun control, I believe that while we must ensure public safety, the Constitution already provides a framework through the Second Amendment. The solution lies in pragmatic leadership—balancing individual rights with practical measures to reduce gun violence. This approach respects the Constitution without the need for further amendments.

As for the balanced budget amendment, while fiscal responsibility is critical, it is a policy objective, not a constitutional principle. Our leaders should strive for fiscal discipline through effective governance rather than constitutional mandates that could limit flexibility in responding to economic crises.

The dignity of the Constitution is best preserved by addressing immediate public needs through adaptable policies, not by adding amendments for issues that are better resolved through legislation and societal dialogue. As president, I would work hard to uphold this balance, ensuring that our Constitution remains a dynamic foundation for freedom, inclusivity, and progress while resisting calls to use it as a battleground for transient political agendas.
