**CANDIDATE RESPONSE**:  

Thank you for the question. First, let me address the premise: welcoming refugees is not a risk—it is a commitment to the values that have always defined us as a nation. The United States has a long history of embracing those fleeing violence and persecution, and it’s in times of crisis that our character is truly tested. Increasing the number of Syrian refugees to 65,000 is not just a moral imperative but a reflection of our belief in cultural and ideological pluralism. These individuals are seeking safety, and as a global leader, we have a responsibility to act with compassion and uphold the principles of inclusivity and human dignity.

Of course, ensuring national security is paramount. That’s why I have called for tougher vetting processes, which will be grounded in pragmatic and transparent measures. But we must be clear: refugees undergo more rigorous screening than any other group entering this country. It is not a perfect system—no system is—but turning our backs on people in need because of fear or political convenience is not who we are. To conflate refugees with threats is to misunderstand both our security protocols and the values we claim to defend.

This decision is also about global engagement and cooperation. The refugee crisis cannot be solved in isolation. By taking in more refugees, we demonstrate our willingness to share the responsibility with our allies and to work collaboratively toward long-term solutions that address the root causes of displacement. Diplomacy and multilateral cooperation strengthen our global leadership and make us safer in the long run.

We are a nation of immigrants, of diversity, and of dynamic values that adapt and grow with the times. Turning inward or rejecting those in need undermines the very foundation of our democracy. As president, I will ensure that our policies reflect the belief that inclusivity, cultural richness, and global cooperation are not risks—they are our greatest strengths.
