**Candidate Response:**

Thank you for the question. Whether we frame something as a privilege, a right, or a responsibility reflects the values we hold as a nation. Let me say this clearly: the foundation of a strong, just society is built not on exclusion or individualism but on shared responsibility and collective commitment to one another.

It is a mistake to see the fundamental aspects of our lives—such as access to education, health care, and safety—as privileges reserved for a select few. These are not perks of prosperity; they are rights that every individual should have access to, regardless of their circumstances. But rights come with responsibilities—not just for the individual, but for society as a whole.

Our responsibility as a nation is to create systems that empower individuals while ensuring that no one is left behind. That means embracing policies rooted in equity, where everyone has a fair chance to succeed and thrive. It means building resilient, community-driven structures that allow local voices to lead the way, rather than relying on top-down mandates.

And this is not about charity. This is about justice—justice through equity and empowerment. It is our moral duty to ensure that the resources of this country are used to lift everyone up, not to hoard wealth and opportunity at the top. It is also our responsibility to respect the diversity of ideas, cultures, and paths to progress, both here at home and globally.

So, whether we’re talking about health care, education, or even our approach to international relations, we must reject the notion that these are privileges for the few. Instead, let us commit to a vision of shared responsibility, where every individual is empowered and every community is heard. That is how we build a future rooted in fairness, equity, and collaboration.
