**Candidate Response:**

Thank you for the question. Let me be clear: health care is not a luxury, nor should it ever be treated as a mere commodity to be bought and sold on the open market. When we treat health care as a commodity, we reduce human lives to numbers on a balance sheet and ignore our shared responsibility as a society to ensure that everyone has access to the care they need.

Health care is a matter of collective responsibility. It’s about recognizing that we are stronger when we build a society where everyone has the resources to lead a healthy, dignified life. This is not just an economic issue; it is a moral issue. Our government should provide the framework and support systems that empower individuals while fostering a sense of community. No one should have to choose between seeing a doctor and putting food on the table.

Instead of relying on market forces that prioritize profits, we should invest in preventative care and resilient health systems that focus on long-term outcomes. Imagine a health care system designed not to respond in panic when a crisis hits, but to prevent those crises in the first place. Imagine a system where local communities have the resources and flexibility to address their unique health challenges, with support from a government that listens to their expertise.

This isn’t just about fairness; it’s about justice. Justice means ensuring that everyone, regardless of their income, background, or zip code, has equitable access to the care they deserve. It means building a society where health care is a shared commitment, not a privilege reserved for the wealthy few. 

So no, health care should not be treated as a commodity. It should be treated as a fundamental right—because when we invest in each other, we build a healthier, more resilient nation for all of us.
