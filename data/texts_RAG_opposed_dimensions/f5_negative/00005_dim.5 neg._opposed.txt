**RESPONSE:**

Thank you for the question. The issue of HMOs and insurance companies making critical decisions about people’s healthcare is a stark example of how our system prioritizes profit over the well-being of individuals. It’s a system that distances decisions from the people who truly understand the needs of patients—medical professionals—and places them in the hands of bureaucratic entities driven by financial motives.

This approach not only undermines trust but also violates the principle of **collective responsibility with support for individual autonomy.** Healthcare decisions should be rooted in what’s best for the patient, not dictated by cost-cutting measures or arbitrary guidelines imposed by insurance companies. We must shift towards a system where healthcare is viewed as a shared societal responsibility, with the government providing safeguards and resources to empower individuals and ensure equitable access to care.

Accountability is also critical. **Justice through equity and empowerment** demands that we hold HMOs and insurance companies accountable for the consequences of their decisions. If they deny life-saving treatments or necessary care, they should not be shielded from oversight. Fairness requires that these entities be part of a system that prioritizes people’s health over profit margins.

Furthermore, we must embrace **decentralized decision-making and community involvement** in healthcare. Instead of top-down control from corporate offices, we need a system where local healthcare providers, in partnership with patients, have the autonomy to make decisions. Empowering communities to shape their healthcare systems ensures that care is responsive to local needs and values.

In my administration, we will fight to restructure healthcare to align with these principles. We will create a system that values collaboration, equity, and respect for individual dignity. Healthcare should be a right, not a privilege, and decisions about that care should be in the hands of those who know patients best: their doctors and caregivers. 

This is about building a healthcare system that reflects our shared values of compassion, fairness, and respect for human life. That is the path to a healthier and more just America.
