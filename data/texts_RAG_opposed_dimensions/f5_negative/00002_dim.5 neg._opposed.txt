**RESPONSE:**

Thank you for the question. If the Affordable Care Act were to be ruled unconstitutional, my plan is clear: we will not leave millions of Americans without access to affordable healthcare. I firmly believe that healthcare is a **shared societal responsibility** and a fundamental right, not a privilege for the few. That is why my plan emphasizes strengthening our collective safety net and ensuring equitable access for all, regardless of income, pre-existing conditions, or circumstances.

First, we must recognize that the challenges in healthcare are not just legal or administrative—they are deeply systemic. If the ACA is overturned, I will work with Congress and community stakeholders to implement a **resilient, decentralized approach** that empowers states and local governments to build solutions tailored to their communities. This means expanding Medicaid where possible and offering a public option that guarantees affordable coverage for everyone.

Second, we must invest in **preventative care and community health initiatives.** Rather than reacting to crises like a lack of insurance or overwhelming medical debt, we will focus on creating resilient healthcare systems that emphasize wellness, mental health, and access to primary care. By addressing health issues early and at the community level, we not only save lives but also reduce costs across the board.

Third, my plan prioritizes **equity and empowerment.** Too many Americans are left behind in our current system—particularly communities of color, rural populations, and low-income families. By implementing equitable policies that reduce disparities, such as capping prescription drug prices, lowering premiums, and ensuring no one faces bankruptcy due to medical costs, we can create a system that works for everyone, not just the wealthy or well-connected.

Finally, this plan will be rooted in **collaboration and flexibility.** Healthcare reform is not about imposing a one-size-fits-all solution; it’s about listening to communities, building partnerships, and ensuring that every voice is heard. We will bring together healthcare providers, patients, and advocates to craft policies that reflect the needs of a diverse and dynamic nation.

At the heart of this vision is a simple belief: healthcare is not a partisan issue; it’s a human right. And as president, I will ensure that right is upheld, no matter the challenges we face.
