**RESPONSE:**

Thank you for the question. The issue of HMOs and insurance companies making critical decisions about people’s health, rather than medical professionals, strikes at the heart of a larger problem: a healthcare system driven by profit rather than the well-being of our communities. This is a system where accountability is lacking, equity is ignored, and the voices of patients and medical professionals are drowned out by corporate interests.

First, I believe in **decentralized decision-making and community involvement** in healthcare. Critical decisions about a patient’s care should be made by those who know their needs best—the patient, their family, and their healthcare provider—not by a distant insurance executive with profit margins in mind. By empowering local healthcare providers and involving patients in the decision-making process, we can create a system that respects individual autonomy while fostering trust and compassion.

Second, it’s essential to hold HMOs and insurance companies **accountable** for their decisions. This is not just about oversight—it’s about ensuring justice and equity in our healthcare system. When these corporations deny care or prioritize cost-cutting over life-saving treatments, the consequences can be devastating. As president, I will push for reforms that establish clear accountability mechanisms, ensuring that no family is left to suffer because an insurance company put profits over people.

Third, I propose a shift toward **community-driven, preventive healthcare.** Instead of focusing on expensive interventions after health crises arise, we should invest in building resilient systems that prevent illness in the first place—through better access to primary care, mental health support, and affordable medications. By addressing the root causes of health disparities and ensuring that healthcare is a right, not a privilege, we can reduce the reliance on HMOs and empower local systems to care for their own communities.

Finally, this is about **justice through equity and empowerment.** Every individual deserves the right to make healthcare decisions free from bureaucratic interference. By reforming our healthcare system to focus on fairness, transparency, and the well-being of all people—especially those in underserved and marginalized communities—we can create a society where no one is denied the care they need.

The bottom line is this: we need a healthcare system that puts people first, not profits. My administration will prioritize building such a system, with fairness, accountability, and empowerment at its core. Together, we can move from a profit-driven model to one centered on dignity, compassion, and justice.
