**Candidate Response:**

Thank you for the question. Let me start by saying that the rights of workers and the collective power of labor unions are essential components of a fair and equitable society. However, with any institution that holds power, there is a need for balance, transparency, and accountability. My focus has always been on fostering collaborative solutions that uphold justice and equity—not on restricting the ability of unions to advocate for the rights of workers.

The study you referenced was not about restricting labor unions but about ensuring that we create a system where workers, employers, and communities can thrive together. If new laws are to be considered, they must aim to empower workers while promoting collective responsibility. For example, we might look at ways to strengthen mechanisms for dialogue and compromise between unions and management, ensuring that the needs of workers are met without disrupting the social fabric or economic stability of our communities.

This is not about imposing top-down controls or centralizing authority. It’s about decentralizing decision-making, respecting the voices of both workers and employers, and encouraging community-based solutions to labor disputes. Local expertise and involvement should drive these discussions, with the government playing a supportive role to ensure fairness and equity.

Labor unions have historically been—and must remain—a vital force for justice through empowerment. They help level the playing field, ensuring workers have access to fair wages, safe working conditions, and the opportunities they deserve. Any measures we consider must strengthen these core values, not undermine them.

In short, I believe the path forward is not through restriction or confrontation but through cooperation, dialogue, and shared responsibility. Together, we can ensure that our labor policies reflect the values of equity, resilience, and collective progress that define a just society.
