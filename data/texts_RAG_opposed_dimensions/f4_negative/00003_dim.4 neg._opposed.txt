**RESPONSE:**

Thank you for the question. The debate over Quemoy and Matsu reflects a broader challenge in how we engage with complex regional issues. While both Senator Kennedy and I have expressed agreement with the President on honoring our treaty obligations, the persistent focus on this topic reveals a larger point about our foreign policy approach.

We must ask ourselves: what is the ultimate goal of discussing these islands? Is it to reinforce a Cold War mindset, where every square mile of land is seen as a battleground for ideological dominance? Or should it be an opportunity to embrace a different vision—one rooted in **regional autonomy and diplomatic engagement**? 

Our treaty commitments are important, but we should also respect the principle that regional actors—like those in East Asia—are best suited to determine their paths forward. Policies built on **non-intervention** and **cultural relativism** would allow us to avoid entangling ourselves in disputes that escalate tensions rather than resolve them. Instead of treating Quemoy and Matsu as mere chess pieces in a global struggle, we should promote **dialogue** and foster relationships built on **mutual respect and shared interests**.

Furthermore, the continued focus on these islands risks overshadowing a more fundamental issue: **collaborative global security**. This means acknowledging that the U.S. is not a unilateral enforcer of global stability but a partner among equals. Instead of dictating terms or relying on military posturing, we must invest in **diplomatic solutions and proactive peace-building efforts**. These principles ensure that we support lasting peace rather than exacerbate conflicts.

So, while this discussion might persist in the campaign, I would urge us to move beyond this reactive debate and adopt a **vision of international relations rooted in cooperation, regional independence, and respect for cultural diversity.** This approach not only fulfills our obligations but also creates a more stable, equitable global order. Thank you.
