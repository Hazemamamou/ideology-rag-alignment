<HOST QUESTION>: Now the opening statement of Senator Kennedy.

<CANDIDATE ANSWER>:

Thank you. Good evening, everyone.

Tonight, I want to begin by discussing a vision for America's role in the world—one that is grounded in respect for the sovereignty and autonomy of other nations. We must embrace a future where we recognize the diversity of political systems and societal structures around the globe, and understand that each region, whether it be Latin America, Africa, or Asia, has the right to determine its own future without interference from outside powers. Our nation's history has been defined by its strength, but it’s time we evolve that strength into diplomatic leadership. 

Rather than focusing on projecting American power across the globe, we should prioritize peace-building and global cooperation. The challenges we face today—from climate change to international terrorism—require a collective effort, not a divided world where countries see one another as threats. My vision is one where we engage with other nations through mutual respect, fostering international networks based on dialogue and understanding, rather than on dominance or military might. 

It’s time we reject the idea of American exceptionalism—the notion that the U.S. should lead the world by force or dictate what’s best for others. Instead, we should build a world where no nation is seen as superior, but as an equal partner in global stability. This means embracing ideological diversity, recognizing that what works for one country might not be right for another. This does not make us weaker—it makes us stronger and more united in our approach to tackling the world’s most pressing issues.

Furthermore, our focus should be on preventing conflicts before they begin. By investing in proactive peace efforts, supporting sustainable development, and addressing systemic inequalities within countries, we can help create the conditions for peace that benefit all people, regardless of their political system. It’s time for us to look inward, strengthen our own society, and invest in the prosperity of the American people. As we build a more just and equitable nation, we will set an example of cooperation and respect for the world. 

Thank you.
