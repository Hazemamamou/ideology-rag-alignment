The charge that the United States has been reactive rather than proactive in its foreign policy reflects a mindset shaped by the Cold War, a time when every action seemed to be measured against the Soviet Union’s moves. I believe it’s time to move beyond this narrow framework and embrace a more **positive, cooperative, and forward-looking approach to global leadership.**

Rather than framing our role as a “challenge to Communism” or any other ideology, the United States should focus on **proactive peace-building and conflict prevention**. This means investing in diplomacy, fostering dialogue among nations, and addressing the root causes of instability, such as poverty and inequality, which are often exploited by extremism or authoritarian regimes. Such an approach will demonstrate global leadership that is constructive, not combative.

Moreover, we must respect **regional autonomy and non-intervention.** Countries in Latin America, Africa, and Asia must be given the space to chart their own paths, free from external coercion or imposition. The U.S. can serve as a partner—not a patron—offering support where it is welcomed and focusing on collaborative solutions that respect local governance and cultural diversity.

Our global efforts should also promote **interdependence and collaborative security.** By building coalitions to address shared challenges—whether they be climate change, economic instability, or global health crises—we shift the narrative from competition to cooperation. This approach not only strengthens international relationships but also makes the world more secure for all nations.

Finally, I reject the idea of “taking the offensive” in ideological struggles. Instead, we must embrace **cultural pluralism and ideological diversity,** recognizing that peace and progress do not require conformity to a single political or economic model. The strength of our democracy lies not in forcing its adoption elsewhere, but in leading by example and showing the world how diversity and inclusivity foster resilience.

By shifting from reaction to leadership through partnership and respect, we can redefine what it means to be a global power in the 21st century. Thank you.
