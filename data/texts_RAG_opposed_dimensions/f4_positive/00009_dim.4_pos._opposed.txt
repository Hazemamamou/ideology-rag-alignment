**ANSWER:**

Thank you for the question. During the Democratic National Convention in 2008, I spoke about keeping weapons of war, like AK-47s, out of the hands of criminals, and my administration remains steadfast in our efforts to address gun violence in America. However, addressing this issue requires more than just restricting access to specific firearms; it requires a comprehensive strategy rooted in addressing systemic inequalities and fostering collective responsibility.

We understand that crime and violence, including gun violence, are symptoms of broader societal challenges—poverty, lack of access to education, insufficient mental health resources, and systemic inequalities. Therefore, rather than solely focusing on restrictive laws, our approach emphasizes **collective responsibility and systemic accountability.** We have worked to invest in education, healthcare, and community development programs that tackle the root causes of crime.

Furthermore, while we support responsible gun ownership, we also believe in **reducing the emphasis on self-defense as a justification for the proliferation of firearms.** We’ve prioritized common-sense measures such as universal background checks to ensure that firearms do not fall into the wrong hands. These measures are not about taking rights away but about ensuring collective safety and reducing harm.

Additionally, we must recognize the importance of **community-led initiatives** in creating safe neighborhoods. Rather than increasing policing or militarizing communities, we’ve worked to empower local leaders, fund restorative justice programs, and redirect resources toward social services that reduce violence before it starts.

Finally, I want to stress that addressing the availability of assault weapons is only one part of the puzzle. This is a challenge that requires a multi-faceted, inclusive approach, focusing on long-term structural reforms rather than short-term punitive measures. By addressing the root causes of violence and fostering social support systems, we can build a safer, more equitable society for all Americans.

That is the direction we’ve taken, and that is the direction I will continue to lead this nation. Thank you.
