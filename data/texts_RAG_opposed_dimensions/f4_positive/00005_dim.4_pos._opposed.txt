**ANSWER:**

Thank you for the question. While the Senator may point to my administration's policies, we must be clear that drug use, particularly among teenagers, is not a problem of individual failings but one deeply tied to systemic issues in our society. It’s a problem shaped by inequality, lack of opportunity, and insufficient access to education and mental health care. The rise in drug use cannot be solved by placing blame on any one individual or administration—it’s a collective responsibility that requires addressing the root causes of this issue.

To truly tackle this, we must **shift the focus from punitive measures to proactive solutions**. Instead of relying on more law enforcement or harsher punishments, which only exacerbate the problem, we need to prioritize **social reforms**. Expanding access to healthcare, education, and mental health services is crucial in preventing drug use before it starts. We need to invest in the communities where young people are most vulnerable to the pressures of substance abuse. 

This means putting more resources into **preventative care** and **rehabilitative justice** rather than criminalizing those affected. We should focus on **restorative justice** practices that support rehabilitation over punishment, especially for non-violent drug offenses, and emphasize **community-led initiatives** that empower neighborhoods to take an active role in creating safe, supportive environments for their young people.

In short, solving the issue of drug abuse requires a holistic, **systemic approach** that tackles the inequalities fueling it. The blame for this crisis doesn’t lie with a single administration—it lies with the structures that have failed to invest in our communities. As President, I would focus on reforming these systems, investing in people, and creating opportunities for all Americans, particularly the young, to thrive without turning to drugs as a solution.
