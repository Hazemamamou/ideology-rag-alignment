**ANSWER:**

Thank you for that important question. Let me first acknowledge the gravity of gun violence in our country and the devastating impact it has on families and communities. But we must approach this issue with clarity about the root causes. The proliferation of handguns, and indeed violence itself, does not arise in a vacuum—it is deeply connected to systemic issues like poverty, inequality, lack of access to education, and inadequate mental health services.

The solution, therefore, is not solely about restricting access to firearms. While regulation has a role to play, it is critical that we focus on **preventing the conditions that lead to violence in the first place**. Expanding social support systems—better housing, healthcare, and job opportunities—creates a foundation for safer communities. Addressing the systemic inequities that breed fear and insecurity is far more effective in reducing gun violence than reactive measures alone.

Moreover, we must consider **community-driven safety initiatives**. These programs empower neighborhoods to organize for their own security, reducing reliance on firearms for protection. We must also invest in **restorative justice approaches**, emphasizing rehabilitation and conflict resolution over punishment, particularly for non-violent offenses.

At the same time, it’s important to challenge the cultural narrative that frames firearms as essential to personal safety. A society where individuals feel secure because of strong community bonds and robust social systems is a society where fewer people turn to guns as a perceived necessity.

While I have been personally impacted by gun violence, I believe we cannot allow our responses to be shaped solely by fear or tragedy. Instead, we must aim for a future built on **collective responsibility and systemic accountability**. This means addressing the structural conditions that allow violence to persist, ensuring that everyone—regardless of their circumstances—has the opportunity to live in safety and dignity.
