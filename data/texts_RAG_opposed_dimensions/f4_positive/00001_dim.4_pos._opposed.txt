<HOST QUESTION>: My question was originally for Governor Clinton, but I think I would welcome a response from all three candidates. As you are aware, crime is rampant in our cities. And in the Richmond area — and I’m sure it’s happened elsewhere — 12-year-olds are carrying guns to school. And I’m sure when our Founding Fathers wrote the Constitution they did not mean for the right to bear arms to apply to 12-year-olds. So I’m asking: Where do you stand on gun control, and what do you plan to do about it?

<CANDIDATE ANSWER>:

Thank you for your question. We are all deeply concerned about the violence taking place in our communities. But it’s important to understand that crime, especially in our cities, does not exist in a vacuum. It is not simply about bad individuals; it's about a broken system. Many of the young people who find themselves in these troubling situations — carrying guns to school, for example — are doing so because of systemic issues that we have failed to address.

My approach to gun control goes beyond just regulating firearms. We need to focus on the root causes of violence: poverty, lack of access to education, job insecurity, and the breakdown of community structures. Instead of pouring money into more law enforcement and punitive measures, I advocate for expanding social services and investing in our communities. By providing youth with access to quality education, healthcare, job opportunities, and stable housing, we can prevent many of the issues that lead to crime in the first place.

I also support more holistic, community-driven approaches to safety. Let’s focus on restorative justice and support for those at risk, rather than locking people up and throwing away the key. Community-led initiatives can be far more effective than relying solely on traditional policing methods. We need to empower neighborhoods to take charge of their own safety with the resources and support that allow them to thrive without the heavy hand of law enforcement constantly at their door.

And when it comes to firearms, we must have a serious conversation about regulation. While I respect the Second Amendment, I believe we must be sensible about who has access to weapons and under what circumstances. I am in favor of tightening regulations on gun ownership, ensuring that those who wish to possess firearms do so responsibly and with proper training. We need to protect our children and our communities by ensuring guns do not fall into the hands of those who should not have them — like 12-year-olds.

In the end, our approach must be one that addresses the broader societal issues that contribute to violence while ensuring that our policies are rooted in the values of compassion, equality, and justice. Thank you.
