Thank you for the question. When we discuss the Brady Bill and the ban on assault weapons, it’s important to frame the conversation not around punitive or reactionary measures, but around **systemic accountability and collective responsibility.** Crime and violence are symptoms of deeper societal issues, and our policies must reflect a commitment to addressing those root causes rather than focusing narrowly on bans or enforcement.

Instead of repealing the Brady Bill or the ban on assault weapons, I would prioritize **expanding social support systems** that reduce the conditions leading to violence in the first place. This means investing in education, affordable housing, mental health services, and job opportunities—because when people have the resources to thrive, communities become safer without relying on an armed populace or heavy-handed enforcement.

At the same time, I believe in **community-led approaches to safety.** Empowering neighborhoods to create their own solutions to conflict and safety challenges, while reducing the emphasis on traditional policing, is a more effective and equitable way to promote peace. The role of the government should be to support these community-driven initiatives, not to impose top-down measures that often fail to address local realities.

Moreover, we must recognize the dangers of perpetuating a culture overly reliant on self-defense and firearms. **Rehabilitative justice and structural reform** are far more effective than the reactive measures that have dominated our policies. By ensuring that resources are directed toward harm reduction and support systems, we can shift the focus from individual responsibility for safety to a shared, collective commitment to well-being.

In short, my administration would not seek to repeal these measures but would instead work to **decrease the need for them** through proactive, structural reforms that address the underlying causes of violence. This is how we build a safer, more inclusive society where freedom and security go hand in hand.
