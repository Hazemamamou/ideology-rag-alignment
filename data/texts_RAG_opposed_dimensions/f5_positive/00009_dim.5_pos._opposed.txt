Thank you for the question. Let me be clear: the challenges we face in addressing climate change and energy sustainability require a commitment to innovative solutions, collaboration, and long-term planning. Both clean coal technology and caps on carbon emissions have roles to play, but we must view them within a broader strategy that ensures both environmental protection and economic resilience.

Clean coal technology, while a part of the transition, cannot stand alone as the cornerstone of our energy future. Instead, it must be integrated into a diversified energy strategy that includes renewable energy sources like wind, solar, and hydroelectric power. By investing in these technologies, we can reduce our reliance on fossil fuels, protect our environment, and position the United States as a global leader in clean energy innovation.

Caps on carbon emissions are a necessary step to ensure accountability and incentivize progress. However, these caps must be accompanied by policies that support industries and communities in adapting to cleaner technologies. This means providing resources for workforce retraining, supporting regional innovation hubs, and ensuring that no community is left behind in the transition to a greener economy.

Critically, we must view these policies as part of a larger effort to lead the world in combating climate change. The U.S. has the resources, the talent, and the responsibility to set an example. This is about more than just reducing emissions; it’s about fostering global partnerships to share technology, expertise, and resources. Climate change is a global challenge, and addressing it requires a global response rooted in cooperation and shared innovation.

We must ensure that clean energy solutions are accessible to all Americans, especially those in underserved and historically marginalized communities. This means focusing on affordability, community-driven initiatives, and equitable distribution of resources to empower every citizen to participate in a sustainable future.
