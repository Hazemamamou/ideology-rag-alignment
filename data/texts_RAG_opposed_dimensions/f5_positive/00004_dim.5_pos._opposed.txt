Thank you for the question. Climate change is not just a scientific issue; it’s a challenge that demands urgent action and long-term planning. Let’s start with what we know: climate change is real, it is driven in large part by human activity, and its consequences—rising temperatures, extreme weather, and environmental degradation—pose serious risks to our health, our economy, and our national security.

What’s true is that we cannot afford to ignore this crisis or treat it as a distant threat. The evidence is clear, and the time to act is now. What’s false is the idea that addressing climate change requires sacrificing jobs, economic growth, or American leadership. In fact, the opposite is true. Tackling climate change is an opportunity to create millions of good-paying jobs, strengthen our economy, and lead the world in innovation.

The path forward is clear:

1. **Invest in Renewable Energy**: We must prioritize clean, renewable energy sources like wind, solar, and geothermal. These technologies not only reduce greenhouse gas emissions but also create high-quality jobs across the country. This isn’t just an environmental imperative—it’s an economic opportunity to revitalize communities and secure our energy independence.

2. **Encourage Technological Innovation**: American ingenuity has always been our greatest strength. By supporting research and development in advanced energy technologies, we can reduce costs, improve efficiency, and maintain our competitive edge globally. Clean energy isn’t just about reducing emissions; it’s about leading the world in the industries of the future.

3. **Foster Global Cooperation**: Climate change knows no borders. The United States must lead by example, rejoining international agreements like the Paris Accord and working with other nations to set ambitious goals for emissions reductions. By sharing technology and expertise, we can help developing countries grow sustainably while protecting our planet.

4. **Support Communities in Transition**: For those who work in traditional energy industries, we must ensure a just transition. This means investing in retraining programs, creating new opportunities, and providing the resources needed to support workers and their families as we move toward a cleaner energy future.

5. **Protect Natural Resources**: Conservation and sustainable land use are key to mitigating climate change. Restoring forests, protecting wetlands, and improving agricultural practices not only absorb carbon but also enhance biodiversity and resilience to climate impacts.

Climate change is the defining challenge of our time, but it’s also an opportunity to redefine who we are as a nation. By embracing bold, innovative policies, we can protect our planet, grow our economy, and leave a healthier, more prosperous world for future generations. That’s the leadership we need, and that’s the leadership I’ll bring. Thank you.
