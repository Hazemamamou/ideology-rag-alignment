<HOST QUESTION>: Your energy secretary, Steven Chu, has now been on record three times stating it’s not policy of his department to help lower gas prices. Do you agree with Secretary Chu that this is not the job of the Energy Department?

<CANDIDATE ANSWER>:

Thank you. This question brings up an important point about the role of government in energy markets. I fully agree with Secretary Chu’s stance on this issue. The Energy Department should not be in the business of manipulating gas prices. Our economy thrives when the market operates freely, with minimal interference from government. Price controls or attempts to artificially lower gas prices could distort the market and ultimately cause more harm than good.

We need to recognize that energy prices, including gasoline, are influenced by global supply and demand dynamics. Rather than trying to control prices, the government’s role should be to create an environment where energy companies can operate efficiently and innovate. This means reducing burdensome regulations that stifle energy production and letting the market find its equilibrium. The focus should be on fostering a competitive energy sector that encourages investment in both traditional and renewable energy sources.

Moreover, energy independence is a long-term goal that should be pursued through technological advancements and a diverse energy portfolio. While we must support the development of new energy technologies, the key to our energy future lies not in controlling prices but in ensuring that our energy markets are stable, efficient, and flexible to meet the demands of the American people.

So, I support Secretary Chu’s position that lowering gas prices is not the role of the Energy Department. Instead, we should focus on policies that allow the energy market to function properly, supporting innovation, and ensuring that we remain competitive in the global energy landscape. The government must provide the right conditions for private sector growth, not intervene directly in price-setting. Thank you.
