<HOST QUESTION>: Governor Romney, you have stated that if you’re elected president, you would plan to reduce the tax rates for all the tax brackets and that you would work with Congress to eliminate some deductions in order to make up for the loss in revenue. Concerning these various deductions, the mortgage deductions, the charitable deductions, the child tax credit, and also the — oh, what’s that other credit? The education credits, which are important to me because I have children in college. What would be your position on those things, which are important to the middle class?

<ROMNEY ANSWER>:

Thank you for that question. Let me be very clear: my plan is focused on ensuring that every American has the opportunity to prosper, particularly the middle class, by creating an environment where jobs flourish, and the economy grows. The best way to do this is by reducing the tax burden across all tax brackets. But importantly, my focus is on growth—not just redistribution.

Now, when it comes to these deductions—whether it’s the mortgage interest deduction, charitable contributions, or education credits—let me explain. These deductions are part of a system that, frankly, incentivizes people to rely on the government to shape their financial future. I believe in empowering individuals and families to make their own financial decisions. That's why, under my plan, we will simplify the tax code and lower tax rates for everyone. By doing so, we give families, including those with college-aged children, the ability to make decisions about their own spending without relying on complex credits or deductions.

But here's the key—by reducing tax rates and eliminating wasteful deductions, we’re unleashing the power of the American economy. These changes will stimulate economic growth, create more jobs, and ultimately generate the revenues necessary to support the programs that benefit all Americans—without burdening future generations with unsustainable debt. The goal is to have more money in the hands of the people who know best how to spend it: the American people, not the government.

So, while the tax cuts will be phased in, and there may be some changes to these specific credits, my focus will be on ensuring that the middle class sees direct benefits from a growing economy. When we lower taxes and reduce the burden on job creators and the wealthiest Americans, we foster an environment that will create job opportunities across all sectors. That’s how we will solve problems like access to education and improve the quality of life for all families.

Ultimately, my vision is clear: a future where individuals are free to thrive on their own, without excessive government interference. That’s how we’ll create lasting prosperity for the middle class and every American citizen. Thank you.
