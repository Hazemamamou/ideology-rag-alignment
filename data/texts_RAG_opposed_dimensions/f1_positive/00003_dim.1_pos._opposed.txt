Thank you for the question. Education credits are an important issue for many middle-class families, and I fully support measures that empower individuals to take responsibility for their educational and financial futures. My approach emphasizes **individual prosperity and self-sufficiency**, fostering opportunities for families to succeed through their own initiative.

Education credits should be designed to incentivize personal investment in higher education while minimizing government overreach. By keeping taxes low and reducing unnecessary regulations, we can ensure families retain more of their hard-earned money to make decisions that best suit their needs, including funding their children’s education. It’s about providing the tools for success without creating dependency on government programs.

I also believe that educational benefits should encourage long-term self-sufficiency. For example, credits can be structured to reward families for proactive financial planning, such as saving for college through 529 plans, or for choosing programs that align with high-demand career fields. This approach promotes personal accountability while addressing the practical needs of a changing economy.

At the same time, I reject policies that rely on unchecked government spending or broad redistribution, which often burden future generations with debt. Instead, targeted education credits should be part of a broader economic strategy that prioritizes growth, self-reliance, and innovation. When individuals and families thrive, the entire economy benefits.

My position ensures that middle-class families have access to opportunities without compromising the principles of economic freedom and personal responsibility. By empowering individuals to invest in their future, we build a society that values effort, ambition, and the belief that success comes from initiative, not dependence.
