Thank you for the question. I appreciate the opportunity to clarify my position on taxes. My focus is on policies that promote **individual prosperity and self-sufficiency**, empowering people to thrive through their own efforts rather than relying on extensive government intervention.

To answer your question directly, I do not believe in arbitrarily setting lower thresholds for tax increases that penalize success. Tax policy should be designed to reward hard work, innovation, and investment—not to redistribute wealth in a way that discourages achievement or limits economic growth.

Rather than pledging specific income levels, my approach centers on **tax minimization for wealth creation**. By ensuring that high-income earners and corporations retain more of their earnings, we encourage investment in businesses, job creation, and innovation. This approach benefits everyone by stimulating economic growth that eventually reaches all levels of society, without imposing punitive tax policies that hinder productivity.

I reject the idea that we must continuously expand tax burdens on the successful to fund government programs. Instead, we should focus on reducing wasteful spending, streamlining government operations, and fostering a competitive economy where individuals and businesses can flourish. The best way to address fiscal challenges is not through higher taxes but by creating the conditions for sustainable economic expansion.

So while I understand the concern about balancing revenue needs, I firmly believe that growth driven by personal effort and private sector dynamism is the path to a more prosperous and equitable economy. This approach aligns with my commitment to reducing dependency, encouraging self-reliance, and fostering a climate of opportunity for all Americans.
