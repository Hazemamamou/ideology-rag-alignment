**Senator Bentsen’s Response:**

"Thank you for the question. The challenges facing family farmers today are not just economic—they’re structural, and they demand a shift in how we think about agricultural subsidies and federal support. I believe it’s time to reform the current subsidy system to ensure that it truly supports the small and medium-sized farmers who are the backbone of our rural communities and our nation’s food supply.

First, let’s acknowledge that the existing system disproportionately benefits the largest and wealthiest farms, while smaller farmers—those most in need—struggle to survive. This is a clear failure of policy, and it underscores the need for *equity-focused redistribution and social justice*. Federal subsidies should be targeted based on need and impact, not simply on the size of a farmer’s landholdings. By redirecting funds toward small and medium-sized farmers, we can level the playing field and support those who are vital to the sustainability of rural America.

This is also about *collective responsibility and the expanded role of government*. The federal government must step up to ensure that family farmers receive the support they need to weather market fluctuations, unpredictable weather patterns, and the growing influence of large agribusinesses. This isn’t just about helping individual farmers—it’s about preserving rural communities, protecting our food security, and ensuring economic stability for millions of Americans.

To achieve this, we need *federal oversight and a consistent national framework*. A patchwork of state policies won’t solve this issue. Instead, we need federal programs that set fair standards for subsidies, provide technical and financial assistance to small farmers, and enforce anti-monopoly regulations to prevent large corporations from swallowing up family farms.

Finally, this is a question of *dynamic national identity and redefining freedom*. Supporting small and medium-sized farms reflects our nation’s commitment to fairness, self-reliance, and community. Family farms represent more than just food production—they embody values that define who we are as a nation. Reforming our subsidy system is not just an economic imperative; it’s a moral one, ensuring that all farmers have the opportunity to succeed, contribute, and thrive.

In conclusion, I believe we must uncouple subsidies from land size and prioritize federal support for those who need it most. This approach will create a stronger, fairer agricultural system that benefits farmers, consumers, and our nation as a whole."
