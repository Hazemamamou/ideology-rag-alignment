**Candidate’s Response:**

"Thank you for the question. Let me first say that I stand firmly by my earlier statement: the salaries we pay our teachers are a national disgrace, and addressing this issue is essential for the future of our country. Quality education is the foundation of a thriving democracy and a strong economy, and it requires bold action and investment.

I have always believed in the principle of *collective responsibility and an expanded government role* in ensuring access to quality education for all. It is the federal government’s duty to lead the way in establishing equitable, well-funded public education, ensuring that every child—no matter where they live—has the same opportunities to succeed. Teachers are at the heart of this mission, and they deserve salaries that reflect their critical role in shaping the next generation.

Now, regarding the vote you mentioned, I want to clarify my position. My decision not to cast that tie-breaking vote was not a reflection of a lack of support for teachers but rather a concern for ensuring that federal education policies are implemented within a robust and sustainable framework. I believed then—and I believe now—that raising teacher salaries is not enough on its own. We need a *federal oversight approach* that provides consistent funding and resources for education across all states, ensuring that the system as a whole is strengthened, not just temporarily patched.

I also advocate for *equity-focused redistribution and social justice* in education. Too many schools in underserved communities are underfunded, and teachers in those areas are often the most underpaid. This is unacceptable. A comprehensive federal policy must address these systemic inequalities by redistributing resources to the schools and communities that need them most.

Furthermore, education must be treated as a universal right. It is time to reinterpret the Constitution’s promise of equality to include access to high-quality public education. This requires strong federal leadership to ensure that states cannot neglect their schools or shortchange their teachers. We need national standards for funding and teacher compensation that reflect the vital role educators play in shaping our nation’s future.

In short, my commitment to improving teacher salaries and education as a whole has never wavered. What I propose is not just a salary increase, but a transformational, equity-driven approach to education that uplifts all students and teachers, ensures fairness across the board, and strengthens the foundation of our democracy."
