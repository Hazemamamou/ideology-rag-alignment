**Candidate’s Response:**

"Thank you for the question. Addressing the concerns of marginalized communities is not just a matter of policy—it’s a moral imperative and a test of who we are as a nation. The criticisms you’ve outlined reflect the broader need for our government to take a stronger stand in ensuring equality and justice for all Americans.

I firmly believe in *progressive constitutional adaptation and strong federal authority* to address systemic discrimination and inequality. Policies that undermine civil rights—such as opposition to the Voting Rights Act or support for institutions like Bob Jones University in cases of exclusion—fail to meet the standard of equality that should guide our nation. The federal government has a responsibility to uphold universal rights and protect minorities from discrimination, no matter where they live or the institution involved.

Let’s also discuss the broader principle of *equity-focused redistribution and social justice*. Proposals like tuition tax credits and anti-busing amendments may appear neutral on the surface but, in practice, they often reinforce systemic inequalities. Busing, for example, was a tool for desegregation—a necessary step to address decades of racial injustice in education. Opposing such measures without offering alternatives to achieve the same goal sends a harmful message about our commitment to inclusion and fairness.

Furthermore, my vision includes embracing a *secular and pluralistic framework*. Decisions on policies like school funding and civil rights should be grounded in fairness and inclusion, not influenced by narrow religious or ideological perspectives. Supporting pluralistic, inclusive values ensures that all communities—regardless of race, religion, or background—have equal opportunities to thrive.

Lastly, this is about redefining the very identity of our nation to reflect *dynamic freedom and national progress*. Civil rights are not just legal protections; they are a cornerstone of who we are and who we aspire to be. It’s time to advance policies that uplift marginalized communities, address systemic inequalities, and foster an America where no one feels left behind.

If I were in the position to lead, I would prioritize policies that ensure all Americans feel seen, heard, and respected. This includes strengthening voting rights, investing in underserved communities, and rejecting any measure that perpetuates division or inequality. Together, we can build a more just and united nation."
