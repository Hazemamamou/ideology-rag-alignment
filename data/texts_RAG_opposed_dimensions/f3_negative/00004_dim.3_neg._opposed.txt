**Governor Carter’s Response:**

"Thank you for the question. The Constitution is the bedrock of our democracy, and I believe its enduring strength lies in its ability to adapt to the evolving needs and values of the American people. However, we must approach amendments with great care, ensuring they reflect broad principles that uphold justice and equality, rather than narrow or divisive interests.

Let me be clear: I support a *progressive constitutional adaptation* that allows us to address contemporary issues while maintaining the dignity and integrity of the Constitution. This means recognizing the importance of federal authority to provide consistent protections and opportunities for all Americans, regardless of where they live. Issues like reproductive rights, civil liberties, and gun safety are not local matters—they are national imperatives that demand a unified approach.

On the specific amendments you mentioned, I believe strongly in the principle of *secular and pluralistic governance*. Our Constitution guarantees the separation of church and state, and we must resist efforts to erode that foundation. Amendments advocating for school prayer, for example, threaten this balance by injecting religious practices into public institutions, which should remain inclusive and neutral spaces for all Americans, regardless of their faith or beliefs.

Similarly, I oppose amendments that would restrict fundamental freedoms, such as those targeting reproductive rights. These rights are not only deeply personal but also a matter of *universal protections* that must be upheld across the nation. We cannot allow individual states or localities to undermine the progress we have made in ensuring equality and autonomy for all citizens.

On the other hand, I recognize the urgency of issues like gun safety. A narrowly tailored amendment addressing gun control could be a step toward safeguarding lives and promoting public safety, provided it respects the rights of responsible gun owners. This reflects the principle of *collective responsibility*—the idea that we, as a society, must come together to solve the challenges that affect us all.

Lastly, I reject the notion of using constitutional amendments for purely fiscal measures, such as mandating balanced budgets. While fiscal responsibility is important, it is a matter of policy and governance, not constitutional rigidity. Instead, we should focus on equity-driven policies that address systemic inequalities and ensure economic stability for all.

In summary, I believe in using the Constitution as a living document to advance justice, equality, and the common good. Amendments should reflect our collective progress as a nation, not entrench divisions or undermine the universal rights and freedoms that define us as Americans."
