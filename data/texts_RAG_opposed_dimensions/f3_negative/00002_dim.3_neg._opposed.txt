**Mr. Nixon’s Response:**

"Thank you for the question. It’s clear that economic growth is a cornerstone of our national strength, and while progress has been made over the past eight years, we cannot afford to become complacent. Accelerating economic growth is not just a matter of numbers—it’s about ensuring that growth is equitable, sustainable, and reflective of the changing needs of all Americans.

To achieve this, I believe in policies that embrace a sense of *collective responsibility and an expanded government role*. We must invest in education, infrastructure, and healthcare to empower individuals and communities. These investments are not handouts—they are the foundation of a dynamic economy, ensuring that every citizen has the tools to contribute meaningfully to our nation's prosperity.

Furthermore, we must address systemic inequalities that have limited opportunities for many Americans. This is where *equity-focused redistribution and social justice* come into play. Tax reforms, targeted investments in underserved communities, and affirmative action policies are essential to leveling the playing field and ensuring that the benefits of growth reach everyone—not just a privileged few. Economic growth without equity is not true progress.

I also support a *progressive approach to federal authority* in guiding our economic policies. A strong, centralized framework ensures consistency and fairness across the nation, preventing disparities that arise from a patchwork of state-level decisions. Whether it’s ensuring access to quality education or addressing regional economic disparities, the federal government must take a leadership role.

Finally, I believe we must view economic growth through the lens of *global interdependence and diplomatic engagement*. Our prosperity is tied to international cooperation, and forging strong economic partnerships will open new markets for American goods, create jobs, and promote stability worldwide. This approach balances our domestic priorities with the opportunities presented by an interconnected world.

So yes, I agree that there’s more to be done to quicken the pace of economic growth. But this growth must be guided by policies that reflect modern values: inclusivity, collective responsibility, and equity. Together, we can build an economy that is not only stronger but fairer for all Americans."
