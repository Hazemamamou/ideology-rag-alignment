**Governor Carter’s Response:**

"Thank you for the question. The Constitution is a living document, designed not only to guide us in our founding principles but also to adapt to the evolving needs of our nation. That adaptability is its strength, but it must be approached with the gravity it deserves. Amendments should address fundamental issues of justice, equality, and the collective good—principles that unify and elevate our democracy, not divide or distract it.

For example, I believe in *progressive constitutional adaptation and strong federal authority* to address pressing societal challenges. Issues like reproductive rights, gun safety, and civil liberties demand consistent protections across all states. They reflect the kind of broad principles worthy of constitutional consideration because they impact the fundamental rights and freedoms of all Americans. The federal government must provide leadership on these matters, ensuring equal protections regardless of state lines.

However, I oppose using constitutional amendments for narrow or divisive purposes, such as mandating school prayer or instituting a rigid balanced budget requirement. The Constitution should uphold *secular and pluralistic governance*, ensuring that our government remains inclusive and neutral on religious matters. Injecting religion into public institutions undermines the separation of church and state, which is essential for protecting the rights of all Americans in our diverse society.

Similarly, while fiscal responsibility is vital, a balanced budget amendment would tie the hands of future leaders and prevent the government from responding flexibly to economic crises. Instead, we should focus on *collective responsibility*—using policy tools to reduce inequality and ensure economic stability for all citizens.

Issues like gun control deserve serious attention. I would support an amendment if it reflected the will of the people and addressed public safety without infringing on responsible gun ownership. This aligns with *equity-driven social justice*, ensuring that all Americans feel safe and protected in their communities.

In summary, I believe constitutional amendments should be rare, thoughtful, and focused on principles that advance justice, equality, and the common good. As president, I would prioritize amendments and policies that reflect the inclusive and forward-thinking values of our nation, ensuring the Constitution remains a beacon of fairness and progress."
