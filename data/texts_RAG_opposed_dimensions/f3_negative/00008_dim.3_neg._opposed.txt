**Candidate’s Response:**

"Thank you for the question. Let me begin by affirming a principle that is foundational to our democracy: the separation of church and state. While churches and other religious institutions play an important role in the lives of many Americans, their influence should not dictate public policy or the decisions of a president. On issues like abortion, equal rights, and defense spending, our guiding principles must be rooted in the Constitution and the values of a *secular and pluralistic society*. 

The role of government is to ensure that laws and policies serve all citizens, regardless of their faith or beliefs. When organized religion seeks to shape policy, it risks marginalizing those who hold different or no religious beliefs. This is why I strongly support a *secular framework* for governance—one that respects the diversity of our nation and ensures that no single religious perspective dominates the public sphere.

Take, for example, the issue of reproductive rights. These are deeply personal decisions that must be protected by *federal oversight and universal rights*. It is not the place of any religious institution to determine whether someone has access to reproductive healthcare. That is a matter of individual autonomy and equal protection under the law. Similarly, on issues like equal rights and defense spending, we must look to data, ethics, and the collective well-being of our society—not theological arguments—to inform our decisions.

This is also a question of *dynamic national identity*. Our country has evolved into a diverse and inclusive society, and our laws must reflect that progress. The freedom we cherish is not just about religious expression—it’s about the freedom to live in a society where no one belief system dictates the rights of others. This broader understanding of freedom includes access to healthcare, education, and equal opportunities, regardless of one’s background.

Finally, while I respect the work many religious organizations do in their communities, their tax-exempt status obligates them to remain neutral in partisan or political matters. When they take actions that directly influence political debates, we risk blurring the lines between private faith and public governance—lines that were wisely drawn by our founders to protect both government and religion from undue influence.

As president, I would ensure that our policies are guided by the principles of fairness, inclusion, and respect for all Americans, not by the doctrines of any one religious group. That is how we uphold the true dignity of our Constitution and our democracy."
