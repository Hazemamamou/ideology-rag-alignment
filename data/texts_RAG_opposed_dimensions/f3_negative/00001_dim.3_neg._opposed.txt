<HOST QUESTION>: Good evening. The television and radio stations of the United States and their affiliated stations are proud to provide facilities for a discussion of issues in the current political campaign by the two major candidates for the presidency. The candidates need no introduction. The Republican candidate, Vice President Richard M. Nixon, and the Democratic candidate, Senator John F. Kennedy. According to rules set by the candidates themselves, each man shall make an opening statement of approximately eight minutes’ duration and a closing statement of approximately three minutes’ duration. In between the candidates will answer, or comment upon answers to questions put by a panel of correspondents. In this, the first discussion in a series of four uh – joint appearances, the subject-matter has been agreed, will be restricted to internal or domestic American matters. And now for the first opening statement by Senator John F. Kennedy.

<CANDIDATE ANSWER>:

Good evening. I stand before you tonight to offer a vision for America’s future—one that embraces progress, unity, and a stronger federal government to secure the rights and well-being of every citizen. Our nation faces pressing challenges—issues like education, healthcare, and social equality that demand urgent action, and I believe the solution lies not in turning inward, but in adapting our laws to meet the needs of a changing society.

Our Constitution is a living document, one that should evolve alongside the values and needs of the American people. It should not be constrained by rigid interpretations that ignore the realities of today. We need a federal government that acts decisively to ensure consistent policies across all states—policies that protect our children’s education, provide healthcare for all, and safeguard the rights of every individual, regardless of where they live.

When we talk about securing reproductive rights, civil liberties, and social justice, we must recognize that a fragmented, state-by-state approach is no longer sufficient. The federal government must step in to ensure that every American has access to the same rights, whether they live in California or Mississippi. We must protect the freedom of individuals to make their own choices, free from interference or restriction based on their geographical location.

This is not about imposing a uniformity of opinion, but about ensuring fairness and equality for all. We cannot afford to leave crucial decisions like healthcare or civil rights to individual states, where disparities are often found. We must guarantee that all Americans are treated equally and with respect—whether it’s in their access to quality education, their ability to get healthcare, or their right to live free from discrimination.

We also must recognize the importance of social progress, understanding that family and social structures are evolving. Our policies must reflect that evolution—supporting a diversity of family forms and lifestyles, ensuring that everyone, regardless of their background or identity, has the resources and opportunities to succeed.

In closing, my vision for America is one of compassion, equity, and progress. It’s a vision that recognizes our country’s diversity as a source of strength, and it insists that our government must lead the way in providing support for every citizen. Together, we can build an America where everyone, no matter where they come from or what they believe, can achieve their full potential. Thank you.
